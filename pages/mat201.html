MAT 201

Calculus II

16 Lectures Intermediate Level Mathematics

Course Overview

Calculus II continues from Calculus I with integral calculus, techniques of integration, and infinite series. This course deepens mathematical maturity while developing practical integration skills essential for advanced mathematics, physics, and engineering applications.

Students will master integration techniques, understand convergence of sequences and series, and apply calculus to compute areas, volumes, and arc lengths. The course emphasizes both computational proficiency and conceptual understanding of integration theory.

Learning Objectives

  • Master techniques of integration including substitution, parts, and partial fractions
  • Understand the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and its applications
  • Compute areas, volumes, and arc lengths using definite integrals
  • Analyze convergence of sequences and infinite series
  • Work with power series and Taylor series expansions
  • Apply integration to physics and engineering problems

Prerequisites

Mathematics: MAT 101 or equivalent calculus background. Strong understanding of derivatives and differential calculus required.

Prior Knowledge: Solid foundation in differential calculus, algebra, and trigonometry.

Lecture Series

Lecture 1

Antiderivatives and Indefinite Integrals

Review of antiderivatives. Basic integration formulas. Initial value problems and applications to motion.

50 minutes
Lecture 2

The Definite Integral

Riemann sums and the definition of definite integral. Properties of definite integrals. Area under curves.

55 minutes
Lecture 3

The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

Part I and Part II of the Fundamental Theorem. Evaluation of definite integrals. Net change theorem.

55 minutes
Lecture 4

Integration by Substitution

u-substitution for indefinite and definite integrals. Trigonometric substitutions. Strategy for choosing substitutions.

60 minutes
Lecture 5

Integration by Parts

Integration by parts formula derivation. Repeated integration by parts. Tabular method for specific integral types.

55 minutes
Lecture 6

Trigonometric Integrals

Integrals of powers of sine and cosine. Integrals involving tangent and secant. Reduction formulas.

60 minutes
Lecture 7

Trigonometric Substitution

Substitutions for expressions with square roots. Completing the square. Applications to integration problems.

55 minutes
Lecture 8

Partial Fractions

Partial fraction decomposition. Integration of rational functions. Linear and quadratic factors.

60 minutes
Lecture 9

Improper Integrals

Integrals with infinite limits. Integrals of unbounded functions. Convergence and divergence tests.

55 minutes
Lecture 10

Applications: Area Between Curves

Computing areas between curves. Integration with respect to x and y. Applications to geometric problems.

50 minutes
Lecture 11

Applications: Volumes of Solids

Disk and washer methods. Shell method. Volumes of revolution and general cross-sectional volumes.

60 minutes
Lecture 12

Arc Length and Surface Area

Arc length formula for curves. Surface area of revolution. Parametric and polar representations.

55 minutes
Lecture 13

Sequences and Series

Convergence of sequences. Infinite series and partial sums. Geometric and telescoping series.

55 minutes
Lecture 14

Convergence Tests for Series

Integral test, comparison tests, ratio test, and root test. Absolute and conditional convergence.

60 minutes
Lecture 15

Power Series

Power series and radius of convergence. Operations with power series. Representation of functions as power series.

55 minutes
Lecture 16

Taylor and Maclaurin Series

Taylor polynomials and Taylor series. Maclaurin series for common functions. Applications and error estimation.

60 minutes

Course Information

This course requires approximately 6-8 hours of study per week including lecture viewing and practice problems.
