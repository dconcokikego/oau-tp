PHY 202

Electromagnetism

16 Lectures Intermediate Level Physics

Course Overview

This course provides a comprehensive study of electricity and magnetism at the intermediate university level. Students will explore electric fields, magnetic fields, and their interactions through Maxwell's equations, culminating in the understanding of electromagnetic waves and their applications in modern technology.

The course emphasizes mathematical rigor while developing physical intuition. Topics include electrostatics, magnetostatics, electromagnetic induction, and wave propagation. Students will learn both theoretical frameworks and practical applications relevant to electrical engineering, wireless communications, and optics.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand electric fields, potentials, and Gauss's law in various contexts
  • Analyze capacitors, dielectrics, and energy storage in electric fields
  • Apply Ampere's law and Biot-Savart law to magnetic field calculations
  • Understand Faraday's law and electromagnetic induction phenomena
  • Master Maxwell's equations in integral and differential forms
  • Analyze electromagnetic wave propagation and properties
  • Apply electromagnetic principles to practical technological applications

Prerequisites

Mathematics: Multivariable calculus including vector calculus, line and surface integrals, and differential equations. Familiarity with partial derivatives required.

Prior Knowledge: PHY 101 or equivalent introduction to physics. Understanding of mechanics, vectors, and basic calculus-based physics problem-solving.

Lecture Series

Lecture 1

Electric Charge and Coulomb's Law

Properties of electric charge, charge conservation, and quantization. Coulomb's law for point charges and superposition principle for multiple charges.

50 minutes
Lecture 2

Electric Fields and Field Lines

Definition of electric field as force per unit charge. Electric field calculations for various charge distributions. Field line visualization and interpretation.

55 minutes
Lecture 3

Gauss's Law

Electric flux and Gauss's law. Applications to symmetric charge distributions including spheres, cylinders, and planes. Relationship to Coulomb's law.

60 minutes
Lecture 4

Electric Potential

Electric potential energy and electric potential. Relationship between potential and electric field. Equipotential surfaces and potential calculations.

55 minutes
Lecture 5

Capacitance and Dielectrics

Definition of capacitance. Parallel plate, cylindrical, and spherical capacitors. Energy storage in capacitors. Dielectric materials and their effects.

55 minutes
Lecture 6

Current and Resistance

Electric current as charge flow. Current density and drift velocity. Resistance, resistivity, and Ohm's law. Power dissipation in resistors.

50 minutes
Lecture 7

DC Circuits

Series and parallel resistor combinations. Kirchhoff's rules for circuit analysis. RC circuits and exponential charging/discharging behavior.

60 minutes
Lecture 8

Magnetic Fields and Forces

Magnetic field definition and sources. Lorentz force on moving charges. Motion of charged particles in magnetic fields. Magnetic force on current-carrying wires.

55 minutes
Lecture 9

Sources of Magnetic Fields

Biot-Savart law for magnetic field calculations. Ampere's law and its applications to symmetric current distributions. Magnetic field of solenoids and toroids.

60 minutes
Lecture 10

Electromagnetic Induction

Faraday's law of electromagnetic induction. Lenz's law and conservation of energy. Motional EMF and induced electric fields.

55 minutes
Lecture 11

Inductance

Self-inductance and mutual inductance. Energy stored in magnetic fields. RL circuits and transient behavior. Inductors in AC circuits.

50 minutes
Lecture 12

AC Circuits and Resonance

Alternating current fundamentals. Phasors and impedance. RLC circuits and resonance phenomena. Power in AC circuits and power factor.

60 minutes
Lecture 13

Maxwell's Equations

Unification of electricity and magnetism through Maxwell's equations. Displacement current and the Ampere-Maxwell law. Symmetry in electromagnetic theory.

60 minutes
Lecture 14

Electromagnetic Waves

Wave equation derivation from Maxwell's equations. Plane electromagnetic waves in vacuum. Energy and momentum of electromagnetic radiation.

55 minutes
Lecture 15

Electromagnetic Spectrum and Applications

The complete electromagnetic spectrum from radio waves to gamma rays. Properties of different regions. Applications in communications, medicine, and technology.

50 minutes
Lecture 16

Electromagnetic Wave Propagation in Media

Wave propagation in conductors and dielectrics. Reflection and refraction at boundaries. Polarization of electromagnetic waves. Introduction to optics principles.

60 minutes

Course Information

This intermediate electromagnetism course requires strong mathematical foundations and approximately 8-10 hours of study per week including lecture viewing and problem-solving.
