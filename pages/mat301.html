MAT 301

Linear Algebra

15 Lectures Advanced Level Mathematics

Course Overview

Linear Algebra explores vector spaces, linear transformations, matrices, and their applications. This course provides essential mathematical foundations for advanced mathematics, computer science, physics, and machine learning.

Students will develop both computational skills and theoretical understanding of linear algebraic structures. The course emphasizes abstraction and proof techniques while maintaining connection to practical applications in data science, computer graphics, and quantum mechanics.

Learning Objectives

  • Understand vector spaces and subspaces with rigorous definitions
  • Master matrix operations and their interpretations as linear transformations
  • Compute determinants and understand their geometric significance
  • Find eigenvalues and eigenvectors for matrix diagonalization
  • Apply linear algebra to solve systems of equations efficiently
  • Understand inner product spaces and orthogonal projections
  • Develop abstract mathematical reasoning and proof skills

Prerequisites

Mathematics: MAT 101 and MAT 201 or equivalent. Comfort with mathematical abstraction and proofs helpful.

Prior Knowledge: Solid calculus background. Exposure to mathematical proofs recommended.

Lecture Series

Lecture 1

Systems of Linear Equations

Introduction to linear systems. Row reduction and echelon forms. Solution sets and geometric interpretation.

55 minutes
Lecture 2

Matrix Operations

Matrix addition, scalar multiplication, and matrix multiplication. Transpose and special matrices. Matrix algebra properties.

50 minutes
Lecture 3

Matrix Inverses

Invertible matrices and conditions for invertibility. Computing matrix inverses. Applications to solving linear systems.

55 minutes
Lecture 4

Determinants

Definition and computation of determinants. Properties of determinants. Determinants and matrix invertibility.

60 minutes
Lecture 5

Vector Spaces

Definition of vector space. Subspaces and spanning sets. Linear independence and dependence.

55 minutes
Lecture 6

Basis and Dimension

Basis for a vector space. Dimension theorem. Coordinates relative to a basis.

60 minutes
Lecture 7

Linear Transformations

Definition and examples of linear transformations. Kernel and range. Matrix representation of linear transformations.

55 minutes
Lecture 8

Matrix of a Linear Transformation

Change of basis and similarity. Matrix representations in different bases. Applications to computer graphics.

60 minutes
Lecture 9

Eigenvalues and Eigenvectors

Characteristic polynomial and eigenvalue computation. Geometric and algebraic multiplicity. Eigenspaces.

60 minutes
Lecture 10

Diagonalization

Diagonalizable matrices. Similarity and diagonalization process. Applications to dynamical systems.

55 minutes
Lecture 11

Inner Product Spaces

Inner products and norms. Orthogonality and orthonormal bases. Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization process.

55 minutes
Lecture 12

Orthogonal Projections

Orthogonal projection onto subspaces. Best approximation theorem. Least squares solutions.

60 minutes
Lecture 13

Symmetric Matrices and Quadratic Forms

Properties of symmetric matrices. Spectral theorem. Quadratic forms and definiteness.

55 minutes
Lecture 14

Singular Value Decomposition

SVD theorem and computation. Applications to data compression and principal component analysis.

60 minutes
Lecture 15

Applications of Linear Algebra

Linear algebra in machine learning, computer graphics, and quantum mechanics. Markov chains and graph theory applications.

55 minutes

Course Information

This advanced course requires strong mathematical foundations and approximately 8-10 hours of study per week.
