MAT 101

Calculus I

14 Lectures Beginner Level Mathematics

Course Overview

Calculus I introduces differential calculus with emphasis on limits, continuity, derivatives, and their applications. This course develops both computational skills and conceptual understanding essential for advanced mathematics, physics, and engineering disciplines.

Through rigorous treatment of fundamental concepts, students will learn to analyze rates of change, optimize functions, and solve real-world problems using calculus techniques. The course balances theoretical foundations with practical problem-solving strategies.

Learning Objectives

  • Master the concept of limits and evaluate limits using various techniques
  • Understand continuity and its implications for function behavior
  • Compute derivatives using definition and standard differentiation rules
  • Apply derivatives to optimization, related rates, and curve sketching
  • Understand the Mean Value Theorem and its applications
  • Develop problem-solving skills in differential calculus contexts

Prerequisites

Mathematics: Strong foundation in algebra, trigonometry, and precalculus. Comfort with functions, graphs, and algebraic manipulations essential.

Lecture Series

Lecture 1

Introduction to Limits

Intuitive understanding of limits. One-sided limits, infinite limits, and limits at infinity. Limit notation and basic limit laws.

50 minutes
Lecture 2

Limit Techniques and Indeterminate Forms

Algebraic techniques for evaluating limits. Factoring, conjugate multiplication, and rationalization. Introduction to indeterminate forms.

55 minutes
Lecture 3

Continuity

Definition of continuity at a point and on an interval. Types of discontinuities. Intermediate Value Theorem and its applications.

50 minutes
Lecture 4

The Derivative: Definition and Interpretation

Derivative as instantaneous rate of change. Geometric interpretation as slope. Formal definition using limits. Differentiability and continuity.

55 minutes
Lecture 5

Differentiation Rules

Power rule, constant multiple rule, sum and difference rules. Product rule and quotient rule. Derivatives of trigonometric functions.

60 minutes
Lecture 6

Chain Rule

Chain rule for composite functions. Multiple applications of chain rule. Derivatives of exponential and logarithmic functions.

55 minutes
Lecture 7

Implicit Differentiation

Differentiation of implicitly defined functions. Related rates problems. Applications to tangent lines and curve analysis.

50 minutes
Lecture 8

Related Rates

Problems involving rates of change of related quantities. Strategy for solving related rates problems. Applications to geometry and physics.

55 minutes
Lecture 9

Linear Approximation and Differentials

Tangent line approximation. Linearization of functions. Differentials and error estimation. Applications to approximation problems.

50 minutes
Lecture 10

Extreme Values and Mean Value Theorem

Maximum and minimum values. Extreme Value Theorem. Rolle's Theorem and Mean Value Theorem. Applications and interpretations.

55 minutes
Lecture 11

First and Second Derivative Tests

Critical points and local extrema. First derivative test. Concavity and inflection points. Second derivative test for optimization.

60 minutes
Lecture 12

Curve Sketching

Comprehensive curve analysis using derivatives. Asymptotes, intervals of increase/decrease, concavity. Complete graph sketching strategy.

55 minutes
Lecture 13

Optimization Problems

Applied optimization in geometric and real-world contexts. Strategy for solving optimization problems. Maximum/minimum value applications.

60 minutes
Lecture 14

Introduction to Integration

Antiderivatives and indefinite integrals. Basic integration formulas. Connection between differentiation and integration preview for Calculus II.

50 minutes

Course Information

This course requires strong algebra skills and approximately 6-8 hours of study per week including lecture viewing and problem practice.
